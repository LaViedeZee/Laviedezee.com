<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><span class="ez-toc-section" id="Introduction_A_Comprehensive_Guide_For_Your_Pilates_Certification_Process"></span>Introduction: A Comprehensive Guide For Your Pilates Certification Process<span class="ez-toc-section-end"></span></h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This article is a guide for those looking to become certified in Pilates. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Like many others, I began my Pilates career as a class-taker: someone who enjoyed attending sessions at my favorite studios (almost to the point of addiction). After a high-pressure desk job and mental health challenges caused me to gain a significant amount of weight, Pilates helped me feel safe and at home in my body again for the first time in years.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I started to feel so healthy, vibrant and full of gratitude that I felt called to continue my journey. I currently hold my Mat Pilates Certification from Mimi Yoga in Miami, FL and am in the process of completing my Contemporary Reformer Pilates Certification with Miami Reformer Academy.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><span class="ez-toc-section" id="Standardized_Pilates_Certification_Program_vs_Studio_Certification_Program"></span>Standardized Pilates Certification Program vs. Studio Certification Program<span class="ez-toc-section-end"></span></h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>There are two main paths to certification</strong>. The <strong>first </strong>is signing up for a nationally or internationally-recognized program like STOTT, BASI (Body Arts and Science International) or Balanced Body. The <strong>second</strong> is opting for one of the many certification programs offered by local studios. Below, we’ll cover the main factors to consider for each path.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><span class="ez-toc-section" id="Standardized_Pilates_Certification_Program"></span>Standardized Pilates Certification Program<span class="ez-toc-section-end"></span></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">One of the main draws of opting for an internationally-recognized Pilates certification program is <strong>brand recognition</strong>. If you plan on making Pilates instruction your primary career, opting for a standardized certification program provides <strong>flexibility</strong> in the event of a move—whether that move is from state to state, country to country, or simply opting to work at another studio in your city. Programs like STOTT, BASI and Balanced Body ensure that you’ll receive the same education no matter where you complete the program and improve the likelihood of finding employment.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">For a list of the top certification programs in the USA, including pricing, <a href="https://pilatesjournal.com/articles/top-pilates-instructor-courses-usa-2025" target="_blank" rel="noopener">visit this article in The Pilates Journal</a>.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><span class="ez-toc-section" id="Studio_Certification_Program"></span>Studio Certification Program<span class="ez-toc-section-end"></span></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Many studios now offer their own training programs. I completed my Mat Pilates certification program through Mimi Yoga, a highly-rated local studio in Miami, FL. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">If you have your heart set on working at a particular studio, this can be a great option, as it allows you to network with management. Studio programs <strong>are often less expensive</strong> and <strong>last for a shorter duration</strong> compared to the standardized certifications mentioned above. </p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><span class="ez-toc-section" id="Types_of_Certifications_Mat_Pilates_Classical_Reformer_Contemporary_Reformer_Etc"></span>Types of Certifications (Mat Pilates, Classical Reformer, Contemporary Reformer, Etc.)<span class="ez-toc-section-end"></span></h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Congratulations! You’ve begun exploring your certification options. When selecting a potential path to take for certification, it helps to have a sense of what your goals are. Below, we cover the different forms of Pilates and compare the benefits each certification offers.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><span class="ez-toc-section" id="Mat_Pilates"></span>Mat Pilates<span class="ez-toc-section-end"></span></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This is perhaps the <strong>most common</strong> form of Pilates on the market, as it requires little to no equipment. It’s also extremely <strong>versatile</strong>, allowing practitioners to teach in a variety of locations and contexts.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Despite not requiring equipment, it is still a very challenging and effective workout—arguably more rigorous than Reformer Pilates. Depending on the program, training can last weeks or months. Virtual training options are more plentiful for Mat Pilates than the other formats listed below. </p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><span class="ez-toc-section" id="Classical_Pilates_Reformer_Chair_Cadillac_Tower_etc"></span>Classical Pilates (Reformer, Chair, Cadillac, Tower, etc.)<span class="ez-toc-section-end"></span></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Classical Pilates is <strong>what most people think of when they think of Pilates</strong>. Classical Pilates broadly refers to the original method of physical conditioning developed by Joseph Pilates, focusing on <strong>specific sequences</strong> <strong>of controlled movements</strong> designed to improve posture, flexibility, and mind-body awareness. Joseph Pilates invented a series of apparatus and machines —including the Pilates Reformer—to help facilitate precise forms of movement. True Classical Pilates is made up of a “closed book” of moves and postures, including popular moves such as “The Hundreds”, though some studios now incorporate new variations on traditional moves.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">When you think of a Classical Pilates studio, images likely spring to mind of various wood-and-leather apparatuses and machines, the most famous of which being the Pilates Reformer. One of the most respected manufacturers of Classical Pilates equipment is <a href="https://amzn.to/46PKMVQ" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Balanced Body</a>. You’ll see their equipment in many Classical Pilates studios.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Classical Pilates training programs are <strong>typically the lengthiest and most costly</strong>, with training modules grouped by machines/apparatuses and lasting a number of months. <strong>Mat Pilates is also a form of Classical Pilates</strong> and is typically taught as a separate module during most training programs. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">While it’s more common to go through a nationally or internationally-recognized program for Classical Pilates, there are “in house” options offered by studios—a notable example being Club Pilates, which has locations around the country. However, training acquired through a franchise is unlikely to be accepted at an independent studio.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><span class="ez-toc-section" id="Contemporary_Pilates"></span>Contemporary Pilates<span class="ez-toc-section-end"></span></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Contemporary Pilates classes are often taught on the <a href="https://amzn.to/3KrH1yr" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Allegro 2 Reformer</a> by Balanced Body, which is advertised as the “sportier” version of a Pilates Reformer. Pilates purists tend to balk at these sort of certifications and classes, but Contemporary Pilates studios are growing in popularity across the company and often cater to a younger, “fitter” crowd.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><span class="ez-toc-section" id="Pilates_%E2%80%9CHybrid%E2%80%9D_Certification_Programs"></span>Pilates “Hybrid” Certification Programs <span class="ez-toc-section-end"></span></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Due to the popularity of Pilates and its offshoot Lagree, there are a number of Pilates “hybrid” studios gaining popularity around the world that feature their own proprietary machines, methods and equipment. These studios typically offer their own training programs, which are<strong> often shorter in duration but franchise-specific</strong>, meaning that you will likely not be able to transfer your certification to other studios outside of that brand.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><span class="ez-toc-section" id="Logistics_Fees_Requirements_Fitness_Level"></span>Logistics (Fees, Requirements, Fitness Level)<span class="ez-toc-section-end"></span></h2>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><span class="ez-toc-section" id="Certification_Tuition_Fees"></span>Certification Tuition Fees<span class="ez-toc-section-end"></span></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Pilates certification <strong>fees vary can widely</strong>, depending on the type of program, duration and level of specialization. As a general rule, Reformer-based Pilates programs cost more and last longer than Mat Pilates programs. However, you can <strong>expect to spend a minimum of four figures</strong> on Mat and Reformer Pilates certification programs.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">If you belong to an underrepresented group, studios may offer special scholarships and programs to subsidize or completely offset your tuition fees. It’s worth inquiring if such scholarships are available for your desired program. </p>



<h4 class="wp-block-heading"><span class="ez-toc-section" id="Additional_Costs"></span>Additional Costs<span class="ez-toc-section-end"></span></h4>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">In addition to tuition fees, “hidden costs” may include <strong>transportation</strong> to and from class for in-person certification programs, <strong>equipment/props</strong>, and <strong>proper attire</strong>. For Classical and Contemporary training programs, <a href="https://shopmy.us/collections/public/2474509" target="_blank" rel="noopener">grip socks are required</a>. Additional “hidden costs” may include extra childcare and/or time off of work, depending on your needs. </p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><span class="ez-toc-section" id="Requirements"></span>Requirements<span class="ez-toc-section-end"></span></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Certification program requirements vary by program, but typically require a mix of lecture, instruction/class programming, self-study and observation. A basic understanding of anatomy is key for instruction, including key modifications for pregnant and injured students.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><span class="ez-toc-section" id="Whats_Next_Employment_Prospects_and_Beyond"></span>What’s Next: Employment Prospects and Beyond<span class="ez-toc-section-end"></span></h2>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><span class="ez-toc-section" id="Becoming_an_Instructor_at_a_Studio"></span>Becoming an Instructor at a Studio<span class="ez-toc-section-end"></span></h3>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The classic career path for those looking to pursue a career in Pilates is to become an instructor at a studio. As mentioned above, it can be helpful to have a sense of where you would like to work and what type of certification is preferred, so you can tailor your certification plan accordingly. Some studios only accept certain types of certification, while others have reciprocity with other studio-specific certification programs. <strong>The easiest and best way to find out is to reach out to studios directly</strong> and ask what type of certification is required for employment.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Beyond certification, securing a role at a particularly popular studio can require extra effort. <strong>Taking classes at the studio regularly</strong> and <strong>cultivating positive relationships with the staff </strong>there can help. Having an established, local personal brand—which can serve as an indicator that you bring your own audience and can fill classes—is also an attractive selling point.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">For studios offering private lessons, having a preexisting client list can also help you secure a role. Oftentimes, instructors can rent a private space for an established fee while they teach their own clients, allowing them to maintain a certain amount of autonomy.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><span class="ez-toc-section" id="Frequently_Asked_Questions"></span>Frequently Asked Questions<span class="ez-toc-section-end"></span></h2>



<ol class="wp-block-list">
<li>What sort of fitness level do I need to complete teacher training?
<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>This will vary depending on the program, but the tagline “Pilates is for every body” is true! Pilates can benefit every body type and be performed by all body types. It’s ok to need certain modifications, even as an instructor!</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>What can I expect in terms of finding employment after completing my training program?
<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Before you invest in your training program, get a sense of what the local market in your city/town is like. Ask around at studios to see if they’re hiring. You could also have a “hidden client base” among your existing network—friends and family members that would love to be trained by someone they trust, love and respect. Start sharing your intention via your in-person and online networks and you’ll start to get little “nibbles” back from the Universe. </li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>How much do Pilates instructors earn?
<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>This varies by city, certification type, studio and private versus group classes, etc. To get a sense of what instructors across the country are self-reporting in terms of earnings, I suggest visiting the <a href="https://www.reddit.com/r/pilates/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Pilates Reddit page</a>.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Will I need insurance?
<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Yes! It’s a good idea to have professional liability insurance, especially if you plan on hosting events or practicing outside of a studio (studios typically have their own insurance). </li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Can I be a part-time instructor?
<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>A increasing number of instructors teach at studios in addition to running a business or working at a corporate job, especially positions that allow for remote work. Balancing both can be a way to enjoy the best of both worlds. </li>
</ul>
</li>
</ol>
