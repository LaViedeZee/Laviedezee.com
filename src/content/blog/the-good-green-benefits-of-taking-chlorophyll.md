<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><span class="ez-toc-section" id="What_is_Chlorophyll"></span>What is Chlorophyll?<span class="ez-toc-section-end"></span></h2>



<p class="has-medium-font-size wp-block-paragraph">Chlorophyll is the assortment of green pigments found in the chloroplasts of plants and algae. As you learned in middle school biology, these green light-absorbing pigments allow plants and algae to perform photosynthesis, which converts the sun’s rays into energy for the organism. </p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><span class="ez-toc-section" id="Benefits_and_Studies"></span>Benefits and Studies<span class="ez-toc-section-end"></span></h2>



<p class="has-medium-font-size wp-block-paragraph">Taking chlorophyll is associated with a host of health benefits, including <a href="https://lpi.oregonstate.edu/mic/dietary-factors/phytochemicals/chlorophyll-chlorophyllin" target="_blank" rel="noopener">detoxification</a> and blood building.  Doula Amanda Gloria Valdes discussed using chlorophyll for blood building <a href="https://laviedezee.com/quarantine-wellness-series-doula/">in our Youtube interview</a> as part of her recommendations for expectant mothers. A 2004 pilot study involving wheatgrass suggested that the high amount of chlorophyll present in the wheatgrass may have improved the quality of red blood cells (<a href="http://www.indianpediatrics.net/july2004/july-716-720.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener">link</a>). The study theorized that the similarity of the atomic structures of chlorophyll and hemoglobin led to an increase in hemoglobin production. Chlorophyll has also been found to be helpful for skin healing in a handful of small studies.</p>
