<h2 class="wp-block-heading">☀️ Sun Tea ☀️</h2>



<p class="has-medium-font-size wp-block-paragraph">There is nothing like a warm cup of tea, especially in the winter months. However, what if I told you there is a different way to experience your favorite teas?  Instead of heating up your tea kettle, you could simply leave a glass out on the windowsill during a sunny day? Enter sun tea.</p>



<p class="has-medium-font-size wp-block-paragraph">Sun tea has been around for ages, and is especially popular in the Southern US where sunshine is generally plentiful and sweet tea is the drink of choice for many. To make milder sweet tea, fans use the sun&#8217;s rays to slowly brew black tea. This method avoids the bitterness that can come from brewing  black tea using hot water. </p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What The Benefits?</h2>



<p class="has-medium-font-size wp-block-paragraph">Even if you are not mixing up a batch of good ole Southern sweet tea, sun-brewed tea can also be a great way to enjoy teas with delicate floral or fruit components that may be overpowered by traditional brewing. You can also infuse your tea with yummy fresh fruit or herbs. The gentle brewing of the sun infuses the water with flavor without &#8220;cooking&#8221; the ingredients in boiling water. I added fresh slices of lemon to my most recent batch.</p>

<p class="has-medium-font-size wp-block-paragraph">It can take a few hours to fully brew, depending on the strength of the sun, but the unique flavor is worth it. Because the tea is prepared using room-temperature water, it is best to consume it the day it is made. Storing it for long periods of time can result in bacteria growth. </p>



<p class="has-medium-font-size wp-block-paragraph">If you are looking to boost your fluid intake, sun-brewed tea is something fun and healthy to add to the mix.</p>
